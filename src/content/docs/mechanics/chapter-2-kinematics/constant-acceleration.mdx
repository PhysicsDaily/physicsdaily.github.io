## Concept & Definition

Calculus handles any motion, but a special case covers an enormous slice of everyday physics: motion in a straight line where the acceleration **never changes**. Cars pulling away from lights, falling stones, balls thrown straight up — all of these are (to good approximation) constant-acceleration problems.

When $a$ is constant, the average and instantaneous accelerations coincide, and the definitions of acceleration and average velocity can be rearranged into a small set of equations that connect position, velocity, acceleration, and time. These are often called the **equations of motion**.

## The Equations of Motion

With initial position $x_0$, initial velocity $v_0$, constant acceleration $a$, and elapsed time $t$, three equations answer nearly every question:

<FormulaCard title="Velocity from Time" label="Equation 1">

$$
v = v_0 + at
$$

where $v$ is the velocity at time $t$, $v_0$ is the initial velocity, and $a$ is the constant acceleration.

</FormulaCard>

<FormulaCard title="Position from Time" label="Equation 2">

$$
x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2
$$

where $x$ is the position at time $t$ and $x_0$ is the initial position of the particle.

</FormulaCard>

<FormulaCard title="Velocity from Position" label="Equation 3">

$$
v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\,(x - x_0)
$$

where $(x - x_0)$ is the displacement from the starting point — the equation to reach for when time is neither given nor asked.

</FormulaCard>

<Callout type="tip" title="Choosing the Right Equation">
List what is given and what is asked. Each equation links four of the five quantities $x$, $v_0$, $v$, $a$, $t$ — pick the one that contains your knowns and your unknown, and leaves out the quantity you neither have nor want.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Pitfall">
These equations are only valid while $a$ is truly constant. The moment acceleration varies — air resistance at high speed, a rocket burning fuel — they stop applying, and the problem needs the full calculus treatment.
</Callout>

<QuickCheck question="A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s². How fast is it moving, and how far has it traveled, after 5 s?">

Velocity from Equation 1 with $v_0 = 0$: $v = 0 + (2)(5) = 10\ \text{m/s}$.

Position from Equation 2 with $x_0 = 0$: $x = 0 + 0 + \tfrac{1}{2}(2)(5^2) = 25\ \text{m}$.

Check with Equation 3: $v^2 = 0 + 2(2)(25) = 100$, so $v = 10\ \text{m/s}$ — consistent.

</QuickCheck>
