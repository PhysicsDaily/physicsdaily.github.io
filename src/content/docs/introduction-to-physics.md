Why does a ball thrown upward slow down, stop, and fall back? Why does a passenger lurch forward when a train brakes? Physics is the study of questions like these — of **matter**, **energy**, **motion**, and the **forces** that connect them. It gives us a precise way to describe the world, test our ideas against evidence, and predict what will happen next.

## How Physics Works

Physics is built on a simple cycle. We observe a phenomenon, propose a model to explain it, express that model in the language of mathematics, and then check its predictions against experiment. A model that survives repeated testing becomes a **law**; one that fails is revised or discarded.

Mathematics is not decoration in this process — it is what makes the predictions exact. Saying "the ball falls faster and faster" is an observation; writing down *how much* faster, at *what rate*, is physics.

## Where We Begin

We start with **mechanics**, the study of how bodies move and why. Every later branch of physics borrows its vocabulary — position, velocity, force, energy — so it is the natural foundation for everything that follows.

Mechanics itself begins with the language we need to describe motion: quantities that have a size *and* a direction. That language is the language of **vectors**.
